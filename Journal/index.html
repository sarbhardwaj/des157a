I read an article in The New York Times called, “10 Intriguing Photographs to Encourage Visual Thinking and Close Reading.” The article discusses that there is a lot that can be interpreted from a picture. It encourages looking closely at details and questioning before jumping to conclusions about what a photo conveys. This intrigued me because pictures can be interpreted in many different ways based on the viewer and the viewer’s past. I am in design so I feel that it is valuable to look at how people view images.

I decided to analyze YouTube because images and interaction play a large role in navigation on the site. One of YouTube’s strengths is the videos thumbnails. Thumbnails convey information about the content of the video prior to the viewer interacting with the thumbnail. This information is conveyed through design elements such as color, text, and composition. I also appreciate the hover previews. They let me preview the content of a video without having to watch it.

I do think there are some negative aspects to YouTube as well. Exaggerated faces, bright colors or misleading thumbnails can be used to gain attention. This can be extremely disorienting. This brought to my attention that visual design can convey more than just attention, it can convey information and allow for appropriate choices to be made.

Best Practices for Overlays

October 15, 2026

I recently read an article by Kathryn Whitenton titled Overuse of Overlays: How to Avoid Misusing Lightboxes. In this article, Whitenton discusses the implementation of overlays in web applications. It appears that overlays can add value to a website, but if done incorrectly they can cause a poor user experience. I have come across some websites that pop up a window as soon as a user enters the website. These pop ups typically ask for some sort of sign up or subscription. These pop ups can cause problems because they can interfere with the task the user initially came to the website for.

Whitenton also talks about the five W's (Who, What, When, Where and Why). When designing an overlay it is important to ask who will use the overlay, what is the purpose of the overlay and if the overlay is actually required. Whitenton also brought up the issue of mobile usage. An overlay that is designed to work well on a desktop may be hard to interact with or to close on a mobile device.

I now realize that because an overlay can be done with JavaScript it does not guarantee a positive user experience for a website. I would like to implement overlays to deliver user requested information, confirmations, and/or important messages instead of pop ups that are not required. Also overlays should be easy to close, accessible and simple.

Best Practices for Form Design

October 6, 2026

I have experience with Java and HTML from a few years back. A lot of this material is review for me. I have used Java and HTML before, but I haven't used all of the material that often in the past few years.

Reviewing this material has allowed me to determine what I remember and what I need to repractice. The majority of the material seems familiar to me thus far. There are however some small things and best practices that I need to review.

Reading Reflection

This article has shown me that effective form design largely deals with providing a user with simple and understandable forms. I've had several years of experience with HTML, CSS and JavaScript. This means that creating forms is a bit of a refresher for me. I haven't placed much thought into form design and the small details that can either help or hinder a user while using the form.

I found providing meaningful labels to form fields, not requesting excessive data, and showing the user when they have made an error to be very important. I have encountered forms on websites that were either confusing or I did not understand what data I had entered wrong.

This article has prompted me to shift my focus from the functionality of the form to the user experience of the form. Google's login forms seem to be designed effectively because they are minimal and only request a small amount of data at each stage. The actions are also made clear so the user does not get overwhelmed by the forms.
